I am an applied mathematician whose research lies at the intersection of graph theory, dynamical systems, and algebraic geometry, with applications in chemical reaction networks and structural identifiability.


I am a Van Loo Independent Postdoctoral Fellow at the University of Michigan Ann Arbor, affiliated with the Department of Mathematics, Michigan Center for Applied and Interdisciplinary Mathematics, and the Center for the Study of Complex Systems. I completed my PhD in Systems Biology at Harvard University advised by Jeremy Gunawardena. My doctoral research focused on using graph-theoretic approaches, namely the linear framework, to characterize the interplay between network topology and system dynamics in biochemical reaction networks. My postdoctoral work has expanded this program toward structural identifiability, model reduction, and convex optimization, in collaboration with Marisa Eisenberg.


A major focus of my research is chemical reaction network theory; in particular, furthering our mathematical understanding of reaction network structure and building bridges between deterministic and stochastic reaction networks. This includes past collaborative work with Polly Yu on disguised toric systems published in published in SIAM Journal of Applied Dynamical Systems.


Previously, I graduated from Middlebury College in 2018 with a B.A. in Mathematics and Biochemistry.
